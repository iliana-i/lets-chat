AI Agent Builder

Taking a platform built for human-authored workflows and reshaping it around agents that act on their own — starting with almost nothing to design against.

The problem

There was no existing pattern for what an "AI agent" should look like inside a low-code workflow platform, and no settled answer for how much autonomy to hand an agent versus a human. [Add a sentence on the specific gap — what could users not do before this that they needed to?]

Getting to a direction

As sole designer on the initiative, I had to go deep into the platform's underlying infrastructure before I could design anything credible — understanding what agents could realistically execute, sense, and hand back to a human, and using that to ground direction through rounds of stakeholder debate and hands-on iteration.

Case Study B

AIRO — Gen AI Orchestration

Tell AIRO the business process you want to solve, in plain language — it builds the workflow for you. Designing the interaction that makes that feel trustworthy, not magic.

The problem

Building a business process automation on Workato meant knowing how to use a low-code builder — a real barrier for the people who best understood the process itself. AIRO closes that gap by letting someone describe what they want in natural language and generating the workflow directly. [Add: what happened before AIRO — how painful/manual/gatekept was this?]

The core design challenge

Turning open-ended intent into something structured, correct, and editable is where most of the hard design work lived. Ambiguous requests need to be resolved without stalling the user; AI-generated workflows need to be legible enough that someone will hand them real business logic, and easy to correct when the system gets it wrong.

Design standards, as a consequence

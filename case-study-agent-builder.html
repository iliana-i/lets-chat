AI Agent Builder: from zero to enterprise platform

How we went from an agent conversation demo with no builder UI to a full-fledged agent ecosystem that lets business users build, deploy, manage, and govern agents at scale.

Company Workato Role Design Lead: Solo IC → Player-coach → Team of 3+ Lifecycle 0 → Beta → GA → Continuous releases
Phase 1

Translate a hacked demo into an actual builder UI, to launch 0→1 in 2 months

Role Solo IC Design Lead Stakeholders 2 executives, 5 eng leads, 1 PM lead, 2 solutions architects Timeline Under 2 months

The ask

Two enterprise architects had put together an impressive demo of an AI agent handling an SDR's workflow end-to-end, but built on a web of deterministic workflows and custom-coded connectors so complex it stumped even our most tenured product specialists.

With our annual conference 2 months out, leadership wanted to turn this into a flagship agentic offering. My job: untangle the hacked demo into a custom agent builder that any non-technical user could use.

Three parallel tracks in <2 months:

  1. A custom agent builder UI for non-technical users, ready to present to 2,000+ customers at our conference.
  2. Agentic as a standalone product, priced apart from the core iPaaS platform, even though it still depended on tools that lived inside that same core platform, like data tables, file storage, and connections.
  3. An immediate rollout to 2–4 early partners, designing a somewhat usable experience around the hacked-together solution as-is, to start selling ahead of competitors.

What I'm proud of

  • Shaping solutions with leadership: Led design decisions at the strategic level, pushing back where needed, raising considerations from other parts of the product, and collaborating directly with execs and leads to shape solutions.
  • Change without losing trust: Reworked a solution the solution architects had built and were proud of, handled with enough sensitivity to keep their buy-in and keep momentum going.
  • Owning ambiguity: Team formed right after a major leadership transition, with no clear final decision-maker. Ended up owning the heavier design calls as a result.
  • Technical fluency: Went deep technically to speak the same language as 5 dev leads, shifting conversations from "you don't understand" to "how can we simplify this together."
  • Systems thinking: Mapped how Genie's infrastructure would translate into a UI-based agent builder, giving the team one shared mental model, and defining agentic workflow patterns before "agentic" was industry language.
Phase 2

Flesh out the initial agent builder into a full-fledged, enterprise-ready platform

Role Player-coach → Managing 2 designers Stakeholders 2 executives, 3 eng leads, 1 PM director + 3 PMs Timeline End 2024 – mid 2025 (alongside other projects)

The ask

The beta worked for simple AI agents. The next ask was to make Agent Studio the most reliable platform for enterprises to deploy customized AI agents (what we call Genies) at scale. We started designing and shipping more and more features: testing, conversation and trace logging, guardrails, and more. But as we did that, the original Genie editor grew harder to read. Users couldn't easily see what each Genie could do or how it was configured. The design ask became: making that complexity legible, not just available.
